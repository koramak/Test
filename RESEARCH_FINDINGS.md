# Program Review — Research Findings & Proposed Rotation

*September 2026. Synthesized from four research tracks (upper chest / biceps / triceps hypertrophy; deadlift & squat strength in a rotating loop; integrated mobility; bodybuilding + strongman hybrid structure). Full agent reports with citations are in the session scratchpad; key sources are listed at the end.*

**Goals:** upper chest (size), biceps & triceps (size), deadlift & squat strength, full-body mobility, bodybuilding + strongman hybrid. **Constraints:** 45–50 min, ≤6 exercises/day (ideally 5), rotating loop ("do the next day"), commercial gym + sled, farmer's handles, log.

**Evidence caveat:** the sandbox could not fetch full texts from any publisher; every claim was confirmed from search extracts of abstracts or reputable secondary summaries, and each agent tagged claims as verified vs recalled. Confidence ratings below reflect that.

## 1. Headline findings

| Finding | Confidence | Consequence for the program |
|---|---|---|
| 30° incline is the best-supported clavicular-pec angle; 45°+ shifts load to front delt. Incline pressing shows preferential upper-chest growth in the one longitudinal study (untrained). | Moderate | Keep both incline presses at 30°; add a third clavicular exposure (incline machine) rather than any flat pressing. |
| **Dips are not an upper-chest exercise** (fiber orientation: sternal/abdominal pec, triceps with the long head shortened). | Moderate | Reframe assisted dips as a triceps/general-chest move on the arms day, kept only because bodyweight dips are a stated goal. |
| **Overhead triceps extension grew the whole triceps ~1.5× more than pushdown-style work** (Maeo 2023, 12 wk, all three heads). EMG says the opposite, which is a known EMG-vs-growth dissociation. | Moderate-high | Overhead extension becomes the triceps anchor (3–4 sets, 8–15). Rope pushdown cut (or zero-cost filler only). |
| Curling with the elbow in the lengthened half of the ROM grows biceps more; lengthened partials ≈ full ROM in trained lifters. Five curl variants are redundant. | Moderate-high | Keep three: incline DB curl (lengthened, Bayesian as its alternate), preacher (heavy), hammer (brachialis/grip). Cut standalone Bayesian and EZ-bar curl. |
| ~10 hard sets/muscle/week captures most hypertrophy; 10–20 is the experienced band. Frequency doesn't matter for size at equal volume; it helps strength slightly. | High | Loop math: sets/week ≈ sets/loop × 0.83. Design targets: chest 10–14, biceps 10–14, triceps 8–14, quads 10–14, hams/glutes ~10, back ≥9. |
| For strength, once per ~8 days per lift is **sufficient but at the floor** of the minimum-effective-dose window. Strength saturates at ~2–3 direct sets per session, so extra sets in the same session don't buy strength — a second exposure does. | High (sufficient) / Moderate (second exposure value, d≈0.27) | Add a **light second exposure** of each main lift, derived from the heavy day's load (~78%), 2 sets, not skippable by default. Replaces the "pause squat, skip when worn down" slot. |
| Heavy (5–8 rep) leg curl / leg extension does not build squat/deadlift strength; strength is task-specific. | Moderate | Revert leg curl to 8–12 and leg extension to 10–15, 1–2 RIR, short rests. Saves ~2 min/session and joint stress. |
| 3-min rests beat 1-min for strength; 2 vs 4–5 min shows no detectable difference. | High / Moderate | 3 min only on the 3 heavy sets of the main lift; 2 min light exposure; 90 s compound accessories; 60–75 s isolation. |
| Supersets (antagonist or upper/lower) save 35–60% time with no chronic strength/size loss at matched volume, but raise internal load — never superset the main lift. Same-muscle compound sets degrade the second exercise. | High | Every day = 1 main lift with full rest + paired accessories. |
| Sled/carry work is concentric-dominant with little muscle damage and no interference with strength/size. Carries triple trunk-extensor activation but have no hypertrophy data (not a trap builder). | Moderate | Dedicated conditioning day is justified; short carries also sprinkled on lifting days, always last (grip fatigue). |
| Log-for-reps the day before heavy log press is the worst ordering. | High (ordering logic) | Conditioning day has no pressing; log AMRAP back-off lives on the power day. |
| **Full-ROM loaded training improves ROM as much as stretching** (Alizadeh 2023, ES 0.73; adding stretching to lifting adds nothing). Bodyweight-only flows did *not* improve ROM. | High | Mobility = loaded end-range work built into warm-ups + 2–3 targeted 20–30 s holds/session, never ≥60 s on a prime mover before a heavy set (−4.6% strength). |
| Antagonist stretching between sets can *increase* reps; agonist stretching reduces them. | Moderate | Fillers during rest target the antagonist or a non-competing joint. |
| No calendar deload evidence; a mid-block week off slightly hurt strength (Coleman 2024). Athletes deload reactively (Bell 2024). | Moderate | Reactive per-lift reset after 3 stalled sessions instead of scheduled deloads. |

## 3a. Implemented loop (what is in the app)

After review the loop was tightened to **5–6 exercises per day** (the lifter counts exercises, not slots) and preacher curls were removed at the lifter's request (no spider curls either). Bayesian cable curl is the primary curl on Upper Power (incline DB curl as its alternate); the heavy strict slot is the EZ-bar curl at 6–10 on Upper Size — lengthened + heavy + hammer covers what preacher did. The cable fly and rear-delt partner were dropped to stay at six.

| Day | Exercises (⇄ = superset) |
|---|---|
| **1 Upper Power** (5) | Log strict press · Seated row · Incline BB 30° · Bayesian curl ⇄ Face pull |
| **2 Lower Power · Deadlift** (6) | Deadlift · Light squat (auto 78%) · Hack squat · Leg curl ⇄ Leg extension · Farmer's walk |
| **3 Upper Size** (6) | Incline DB 30° · Cable row · Incline machine · Overhead extension ⇄ EZ-bar curl · Lateral raise |
| **4 Lower Size · Squat** (6) | Squat 4–6 · Light deadlift (auto 78%) · Leg press · Back extension ⇄ Calves · Sled push |
| **5 Arms + Shoulders** (5) | Assisted dips ⇄ Lat pulldown · Overhead extension ⇄ Hammer curl · Lateral raise |
| **6 Strongman Conditioning** (5) | Sled push · Backward drag · Back extension · Farmer's walk · Suitcase carry |

**Order within a day** (checked after the lifter flagged two incline presses back to back): the main lift always goes first (exercise order drives strength gains — Nunes 2021); two exercises for the same muscle are never adjacent — a pull sits between the two presses on days 1 and 3 so chest and triceps recover (antagonist ordering preserves performance in the second exercise; same-muscle compound sets degrade it — Weakley 2025, Brentano 2017); light second exposures come right after the main lift while the lifter is warm; supersets are antagonist or non-competing pairs; carries and sled are last because grip fatigue would hurt rows and hinges (back extension moves ahead of the carries on day 6).

Each day carries its own 5-minute mobility block on the day-overview screen. Engine rules implemented: derived light exposures, ~2% increments for deadlift/squat, double jump when every set beats the range by 2+, reactive −10% reset after three flat heavy sessions. RPE gate not implemented (no new inputs).

### Loop arithmetic
- Order A→B→C→D→E→F alternates upper/lower; no pressing on F so F→A is clean; deadlift (B) and squat (D) are ≥2 days apart in both directions; each main lift gets a heavy exposure every ~8.4 days and a light exposure ~4 days later.
- Weekly-equivalent hard sets (×0.83): upper chest 10–12 (+ log fractional), triceps ~12, biceps ~12 (incl. pulling), side delts ~6, back ~9–10, quads ~12 + sled, hamstrings/glutes ~10.
- **Fallback:** if deadlift or squat stalls for 3 consecutive heavy exposures, drop F and sprinkle its implements (5-day loop → each lift every 7 days).

## 4. Progression-engine changes (Step 3 code)

The existing double progression is already a performance-driven autoregulator; the evidence says keep it and add what it lacks:

1. **Derived light exposures** — `squat_light` and `deadlift_light` never progress on their own: load = round(0.78 × heavy lift's current working load / 5) × 5.
2. **Number-agnostic increments** for deadlift/squat: max(5, ~2% of load rounded to 5).
3. **Double increment** when every working set beats the top of the range by ≥2 reps (fixes wide ranges stranding the lifter).
4. **Stall reset:** 3 consecutive heavy sessions with no load or rep gain → −10% load, rep target = top of range for 2 sessions, then resume.
5. *(Optional, needs one new input)* **RPE gate:** progress only if the hardest set was ≤ RPE 8.5; hold if ≥ 9.5. Requires a single "hardest set RPE" tap per exercise. Not implemented unless requested.
6. Rest times per exercise updated to the table above.

## 5. Open uncertainties (honest list)
- No study tests 8-day lift spacing; the loop extrapolates from 7-day, volume-equated data (short extrapolation, detraining data make it safe).
- Angle-specific chest evidence is mostly EMG; the one longitudinal incline study is untrained subjects.
- Overhead-extension superiority is one 12-week study in untrained subjects (large, coherent effect; unreplicated in trained lifters).
- Shoulder-extended curl superiority is mechanistic; two 2025–26 direct trials exist but only titles were retrievable.
- Sled hypertrophy is under-studied; programmed as quad work capacity, not a squat substitute.
- Practitioner mobility thresholds (knee-to-wall etc.) are consensus, not validated cutoffs.
